Weight Gain After Marriage: Why It Happens and How to Manage It Together

Gaining weight after marriage is very common, and it's usually because your eating routine changed, not because something is wrong with your body. The good news is habits can be changed slowly, and it's even easier when you do it together.

Why does weight go up?

Many start eating their husband's portion without realising. Dinner gets later, there's more lepak and eating out, and lots of family kenduri. Exercise routines also often stop.

Same plate, different portions

Men usually need more calories. So cook the same menu, but serve different portions. For example, one scoop of rice for you, two scoops for your husband. One scoop of rice is an estimated 150-200 calories.

Cooking at home without a special menu

You don't need to cook two kinds of dishes. Cook ayam masak kicap, stir-fried vegetables and soup, then everyone takes what they need. Reduce the oil bit by bit, and the family usually won't notice.

Activities together

Replace one mamak lepak session with an evening walk in the park. Try badminton, cycling or home workouts together. You still get quality time, just with more movement.

Talk without blaming anyone

Tell your husband what you want to achieve and how he can help. For example, buying fewer sweet drinks for the house. If you're planning a pregnancy or are pregnant, discuss your weight goals with your doctor first.

Try this week

  • Use a smaller plate for yourself this week.
  • Suggest one evening walk with your husband.
  • Keep sweet drinks and snacks away from the coffee table.
  • Set one night a week to eat out, not every night.
  • At a kenduri, take the dishes first, a little rice.
Myth: Some say gaining weight after marriage is normal and can't be avoided. Actually it commonly happens because of changes in habits, and habits can be managed without extreme diets.

FAQ

My husband isn't interested in dieting. What do I do?

You don't need to force him. Focus on your own plate and cook tasty food for everyone. Often your partner starts following when he sees the results.

Every weekend we balik kampung and eat a lot. Any tips?

Enjoy your family's cooking, but take small portions and add vegetable dishes. Move a bit in the kampung, for example an evening walk. On Monday, go straight back to your usual routine.

I'm trying to get pregnant. Can I lose weight?

This needs to be discussed with your doctor because everyone's needs are different. In general, eating balanced meals and staying active is still good, but avoid strict diets without your doctor's advice.
