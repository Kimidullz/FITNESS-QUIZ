Weight Not Dropping But Clothes Getting Looser: What Does It Mean?

Usually it's a good sign. Your body may be losing fat even though the number on the scale hasn't changed much yet.

Read in Malay: versi Bahasa Melayu

The scale isn't the full story

Body weight includes water, muscle, bone, food in your stomach and fat. The number can go up and down 1-2 kg a day just because of water and salt. So looser clothes are often more honest than a single scale reading.

Fat down, muscle up

If you've just started exercising or lifting weights, your body can build a little muscle while losing fat. Muscle is denser, so your body looks smaller even though your weight is about the same. This is called body recomposition.

Effects of your menstrual cycle

Many women hold more water the week before and during their period. Weight can go up temporarily even if you eat as usual. Compare your weight at the same phase of your cycle each month.

Ways to measure besides the scale

Measure your waist once a week. Take front and side photos every two weeks. Notice your work pants, your energy and your strength. All of these show your real progress.

Try this week

  • Measure your waist on the same morning each week.
  • Take progress photos with the same clothes and lighting.
  • Note down your period dates so you understand your weight ups and downs.
  • Calculate your weekly average weight, not daily readings.
Myth: Many think that if the scale doesn't move, their effort is wasted. In reality, looser clothes and a smaller waist mean your body is changing in the right direction.

FAQ

How often should I weigh myself?

2-3 times a week in the morning, after going to the toilet, is enough. Look at the average, not one number.

Why does my weight go up after dinner out?

Restaurant food is usually high in salt and carbs, so your body holds more water. It usually comes back down in 2-3 days if you get back to your routine.

When should I change my eating?

If your average weight and waist measurement don't change at all for 3-4 weeks, try reducing your portions a little or adding more daily steps. If you have other symptoms like feeling very tired, see a doctor.
