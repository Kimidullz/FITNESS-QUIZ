Walking for age 50 and above: how long, how fast, and how to start

Walking is one of the easiest and cheapest forms of exercise. No equipment, no fees. It's good for your heart, blood sugar, blood pressure, mood and sleep. But many people aren't sure how long and how fast is right. Let's make it simple.

Important: See your doctor first if you feel pain or tightness in your chest, unusual shortness of breath, or palpitations when walking a little; if you have ever fainted or often feel dizzy; if your calves hurt when walking and the pain goes away when you rest; or if you have a foot wound that is slow to heal. If you take insulin or diabetes medicine that can lower your sugar, ask your doctor about the risk of low sugar and bring a small sweet snack when walking. Don't change your medicine on your own.

How long: the target and how to start

The usual long-term target suggested is about 150 minutes a week. For example, 30 minutes a day, 5 days a week. But you don't need to hit it straight away. If you rarely walk now, start with 10 minutes a day. Every week, add 5 minutes. You can also split 30 minutes into 3 sessions of 10 minutes, and it's still beneficial.

How fast: use the talk test

No need for a smartwatch. Use the talk test. The right pace is when you can still talk in full sentences, but can't sing comfortably. Your breathing is a bit faster and your body feels warm. If you're panting until you can't talk, slow down. Some days your body feels energetic, some days not. That's normal.

Walking after meals for blood sugar

A light 10 to 15 minute walk after eating can help your muscles use the sugar from your food. This is especially useful after rice or noodles. For example, after lunch at a nasi campur shop, walk one round of the block before going back to the office. After dinner, walk around your taman with your spouse or grandchildren.

Hot weather and safe places

Malaysian weather is hot and humid. Walk early in the morning or late in the afternoon. When it rains or there's haze, walk inside a shopping mall, along your apartment corridor, or at home by marching on the spot. Choose a flat, well-lit route. Wear shoes with soft soles, a hat, and bring water. If you're worried about your balance, walk with a friend.

Take care of your knees and feet

If your knees hurt, choose a flat track and avoid hills first. Short steps are gentler on the knees than long steps. If you have diabetes, check the soles of your feet every day for wounds, blisters or red skin, because small wounds can become serious.

Try this week

  • Today, walk 10 minutes after lunch or dinner.
  • Set 3 fixed days this week for walking, and put them in your phone calendar.
  • Use the talk test: chat with a friend while walking, and if you can't finish a sentence, slow down.
  • Every week, add just 5 minutes until you reach 30 minutes a day.
  • Check your shoes: if the soles are worn out or thin, it's time to change them.

FAQ

Do I need to hit 10,000 steps a day?

Not necessarily. The 10,000 figure is not a must. What's more important is adding from your current level. If you usually do 3,000 steps, try 4,000 first. Every bit extra has its benefits.

Is walking slowly still beneficial?

Yes. Walking slowly is still better than sitting. Once your body gets used to it, try going a bit faster for a few minutes, then slow down again. This is a gentle way to raise your fitness level.

Is walking on a treadmill the same as walking outside?

Both are good. A treadmill is useful when it rains or there's haze, and the surface is flat. Make sure you hold the handles when starting and stopping, and begin at a slow speed.
