HIIT for Beginners: 15-Minute Home Routine, No Equipment

HIIT is a workout that alternates between fast effort and short rests, and the beginner version can be done in 15 minutes in your living room. Follow this routine 2-3 times a week, and start with the no-jump version first, okay.

Works: Full body: thighs, glutes, core, chest and shoulders. It also trains your heart and lungs so your stamina goes up.
For beginners: 2 rounds (15 minutes including warm-up and cool-down), 2-3 times a week with a day in between. On other days, a relaxed walk is enough.

How to do it

  1. Warm up for 3 minutes: march on the spot, roll your shoulders and do slow squats.
  2. Pick 4 moves: squat, fast march on the spot, wall push-up and step jack (jumping jack without jumping).
  3. Do each move for 30 seconds, then rest 30 seconds. 4 moves = 1 round (4 minutes).
  4. Rest 1 minute, then repeat for a second round.
  5. Cool down for 3 minutes: walk slowly and stretch your thighs, calves and shoulders.

Common mistakes

  • Going so fast that your form breaks down. Quality first, speed later.
  • Skipping the warm-up to save time. This makes it easy to strain a muscle or get injured.
  • Doing HIIT every day. Your body needs rest, so every other day is better.

Easier version

Switch to 20 seconds work, 40 seconds rest. Keep all moves no-jump, and do push-ups against a wall or table.

Harder version

Switch to 40 seconds work, 20 seconds rest. Swap in jumping jacks, squat jumps and knee or floor push-ups. Add a third round.

FAQ

Is 15 minutes of HIIT enough to lose weight?

HIIT helps burn energy and build stamina, but fat loss comes from a mix of eating and overall movement. Watch your rice and lauk portions, add daily walking, and your results will be more consistent.

Can I do HIIT right after eating?

It's more comfortable to wait 1-2 hours after a heavy meal. If you just had a light snack, 30-60 minutes is usually okay.

I live in a condo and worry about noise for the neighbours below. Is there a quiet version?

Yes. Use step jacks, fast marching, normal squats and wall push-ups. All no-jump, but your heart still beats fast.
