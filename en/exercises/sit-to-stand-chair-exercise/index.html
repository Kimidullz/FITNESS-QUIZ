Sit to Stand Chair Exercise for Beginners

Sit to stand from a chair is the most beginner-friendly version of the squat, also suitable for those who are heavier or not confident with their knees. All you need is one stable chair and a few minutes a day.

Works: Front thighs, glutes, back thighs and core muscles. It also trains everyday movements like getting up from the sofa, car or toilet.
For beginners: 2-3 sets x 6-12 reps, 3 times a week. Tip: do 5-10 reps every TV ad break or every time you get up from your work desk.

How to do it

  1. Sit at the edge of a stable chair, with the chair against a wall so it doesn't slip.
  2. Place your feet flat on the floor, hip-width apart, slightly behind your knees.
  3. Cross your arms over your chest or reach them forward.
  4. Lean your body slightly forward, then push through the floor and stand up tall.
  5. Squeeze your glutes once you're fully standing.
  6. Sit back down slowly over 2-3 seconds, don't drop down.

Common mistakes

  • Dropping your bum down when sitting. Lower slowly with control, that's where your muscles work the most.
  • Pushing off your thighs with your hands every time. Try using your legs first, use your hands only as help when needed.
  • Knees caving in when standing up. Make sure your knees follow the direction of your toes.

Easier version

Use a higher chair or put a cushion on the chair. You can put your hands on your thighs for a little help.

Harder version

Use a lower chair, lower down over 4 seconds, or stop just before your bum touches the chair (hover) and stand straight back up.

FAQ

Is this suitable for older adults or those who are heavier?

Yes, this is one of the best exercises to start with. Choose a tall, stable chair and have someone nearby if your balance isn't great. If you have health problems, ask your doctor first.

Can I use an office chair with wheels?

Not recommended because the chair can move and you might fall. Use a sturdy dining chair or plastic chair, placed against a wall.

What's the difference between this and a squat?

The movement is almost the same, but the chair gives you a target and a sense of safety. Once you can do 3 sets x 12 easily, you're ready to try a regular squat.
